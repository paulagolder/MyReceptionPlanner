MyReceptionPlanner 101

MyReceptionPlanner plans a celebration of a few events: who comes, where they sit, what they eat, and what to send them. This page takes you through it in the order you would plan a real occasion.

1. Start with the example

The first time the planner starts it opens an example, Romeo and Juliette's Wedding: a ceremony with reserved pews, a Wedding Breakfast at one round table and a Banquet for 67, with menus and seating done. Try anything on it. Occasion ▾ → Open the example… brings back a fresh copy at any time.

2. The occasion and its events

The occasion is the whole celebration (Janet and John's Wedding, 12 May 2026). It has one guest list, shared by its events (the ceremony, the wedding breakfast, the evening party), each with its own room, tables and seating.

  • Occasion ▾ → New occasion… starts one; View occasion… holds its name, date, who messages are from, how to reply, and its emblem.
  • Event ▾ → New event… adds an event; View event… gives its place, time and type: Seated (everyone has a seat), Hall / ceremony (pews open to all, with a few reserved seats) or Party, unreserved (no seats given). Tick Catered when food is served.

3. The guests

Switch to Guests (top left). Guests come in parties: a lead, the one you contact, and their places (Ann Smith+1 until you know the name).

  • New… makes a party. Type the lead's details, or Find in a contacts file…: export your contacts from Google Contacts (or Apple, Outlook) as a vCard and pick from it.
  • Type over a name to change it; add a role (Mother of the bride); double-click Diet for dietary needs.
  • The planner warns you if a name looks like someone already on the list.

4. Invitations

Each guest has a mark for each event, in the Guests view; click it to change it:

(nothing)not invited
○to be invited
●invited: the invitation has been sent
✔accepted
✘declined or cancelled

Sending the Invitation message (step 7) turns rings into dots.

5. Set out the room

In the Event view, with Seating off:

  • Layout ▾ adds round, rectangular, oval or polygon tables, pews and an altar, or a table design such as a T or an E top table, made of the venue's tables pushed together (the designs are in the Table designs folder beside your occasions).
  • Drag to move; drag the round handle to turn; double-click to set the number of places, a name, or make it the head table.
  • Right-click a chair to make it seat 1 or disable it.
  • Tables pushed against each other can be combined: right-click one, or Table ▾, then Combine with…. They then move and turn as one, and the chairs the join blocks are turned off. Each part keeps its name, or what it was called (“Table 4”) as a name; rename the parts as you like (“Window”, “Left”), each shown on its own part, as the caterers serve each part on its own. No two tables can share a name.
  • Table ▾ → Renumber tables… numbers them by where they stand, a combined table counting as one.
  • Layout ▾ → Layout plan (PDF)… is for those setting up the room: the tables to scale with their chairs and names, no guests or seat numbers, a scale bar and how many tables and chairs.
  • Layout ▾ → Approve layout… signs the layout off, with who approved it and the date; the layout plan then says Approved, not Draft. Any later change to the layout (a table moved, turned, added, removed or renamed, or its chairs changed) undoes the approval, shown in red in Progress; putting it back exactly as approved approves it again. Seating guests does not count.

6. Seat the guests

Turn Seating on. Click a table: its seats are listed on the right, in the Table tab. Choose a party or type a name on an empty seat, or right-click a seat to seat someone from the list. The Guests tab lists everyone invited; choose a party to see only its guests, and tick Not seated to see who still needs a place.

7. Menus

For a catered event, Catering ▾:

  • Menu…: the dishes, course by course, with the diets each suits. Paste a menu… takes the caterer's menu copied as text (headings become courses, (V), (GF)… become diets); Import… takes a menu file. Check it all before OK.
  • Menu choices and dietary needs…: each guest's dish for each course; ⚠ marks one that does not suit them.
  • Catering list, Kitchen quantities and Service by table for the caterers, each as a PDF.
  • Export menu… keeps the menu in a file, as XML or as YAML (short, to read and edit), to use again.

8. Write to the guests

Messages ▾ → Write to guests…: choose the event, the message (Invitation, Menu choices, Reminder…) and who to show. Each message is filled in for its party (Dear Count Paris…). Then send by Email, WhatsApp, Letters, Address labels, or make a List to contact people yourself. Change the wording in Message templates….

9. See how far you have got

Occasion ▾ → Progress… shows each stage, from defining the occasion to the last menu choice, with what is left. Click a stage to go and do it. Event ▾ → Progress… lists spare places and guests not yet seated.

10. Print and save

  • Event ▾ → Table plan (PDF)… makes a plan to put up, A4 to A1.
  • Event ▾ → Table list (PDF)… makes a card for one table, its guests in seat order: A5 or A6 for a stand, or folded like a greetings card (A4 to A5, or A5 to A6). All tables makes every table's card at once, as many to a sheet of A4 as fit.
  • Event ▾ → Seat finder (PDF)… makes the board for the door: the room with seat numbers, and everyone by surname with their table and seat (A4 or A3).
  • Event ▾ → Place cards (PDF)… makes a folded card for each seated guest, table by table, 6 to an A4 sheet of perforated card (DECAdry) or 4 to plain card; the back can carry their menu choices and dietary needs for the waiting staff.
  • Occasion ▾ → Save occasion… saves everything. Each occasion is a folder in Documents/MyReceptions; what you print goes in its Output folder.
